Focus Questions

What does cell theory say?
All living organisms are made of cells, cells are the basic unit of structure and function, and new cells come from pre-existing cells by division.
Why are microscopes important in cell biology?
Cells are too small to study with the naked eye, so microscopes made it possible to observe cells, organelles, and later the fine details of membranes.
What is the difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?
Prokaryotic cells are smaller and lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Eukaryotic cells have a nucleus, organelles, and internal membrane systems.
Why does surface area to volume ratio matter?
As cells get larger, volume increases faster than surface area, making exchange of materials across the membrane less efficient.
What is the plasma membrane model?
The fluid mosaic model describes a phospholipid bilayer with proteins, cholesterol, glycolipids, and glycoproteins that control transport and cell communication.
What are passive and active transport?
Passive transport moves substances down gradients without ATP. Active transport moves substances against gradients using ATP and transport proteins.

Focus Questions

Why is ATP called the energy currency of the cell?
ATP stores energy in phosphate bonds and releases small, usable amounts of energy quickly when hydrolysed to ADP and inorganic phosphate.
Where do the stages of aerobic respiration occur?
Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm; the link reaction and Krebs cycle occur in the mitochondrial matrix; electron transport and chemiosmosis occur on the inner mitochondrial membrane.
Why is oxygen important in respiration?
Oxygen is the terminal electron acceptor in the electron transport chain. Without oxygen, the chain stops and ATP production falls sharply.
What is fermentation?
Fermentation allows glycolysis to continue without oxygen by regenerating NAD, producing lactate in animals or ethanol and carbon dioxide in yeast.
What happens in light-dependent photosynthesis?
Light excites electrons in chlorophyll, water is split, ATP is formed, and reduced NADP is produced for the Calvin cycle.
What happens in the Calvin cycle?
Carbon dioxide is fixed to RuBP, reduced using ATP and reduced NADP, and converted into carbohydrate while RuBP is regenerated.
